Below is the initial framework brief for the web team. Contractors managing permitting internally are the primary audience, with a dedicated path for permit expediting companies.

1. Website purpose and design direction

Build permitsmanager.com as the public marketing and customer-acquisition website for Permits Manager.

The site should help visitors:
- Understand the product and identify whether it fits their business.
- Explore relevant features and workflows.
- Request a demo or sign up.
- Sign in to their existing application account.
Use a clean, professional software-product layout with clear typography, generous spacing, and actual application screenshots. Emphasize product workflows over generic construction stock photography. All layouts must work well on desktop and mobile.

2. Global header
Position	Element	Behavior
Left	Permits Manager logo	Link to homepage
Main navigation	Solutions	Dropdown: For Contractors; For Permit Expediters
Main navigation	Features	Link to features overview
Main navigation	How It Works	Link to product walkthrough
Main navigation	About	Link to company/product story
Right	Sign In	Open the existing application login
Right	Get Started	Primary button linking to /signup


Keep Sign In visible and distinct from the sales navigation. On mobile, keep it easily accessible alongside the menu or near the top of the expanded menu.

Prepare a Pricing navigation item, but display it only once pricing and packaging are approved.

3. Homepage layout, from top to bottom
Section	Content and layout
Hero	Left: headline, supporting copy, and two CTAs. Right: a large application screenshot showing a useful dashboard or project view.
Audience selection	Two cards: “Manage Permitting In-House” and “Run a Permit Expediting Business.” Each links to its dedicated solution page.
Core benefits	Three concise benefits: see where work stands; keep documents and responsibilities connected; coordinate follow-up across the team.
Product capabilities	Four feature groups: Manage the Work; Organize Documents; Coordinate People; Track Fees and Reporting.
Workflow walkthrough	Show the progression from project creation through permits, documents, assignments, inspections, and reporting.
Built from real permitting experience	Explain the product’s permit-expediting origins. Use approved screenshots and customer proof as available.
Final conversion section	“Bring your permitting operation into one workspace.” Buttons: Get Started and Request a Demo.


Working hero copy:
Take control of your permitting operation.

Manage projects, permits, inspections, documents, and team responsibilities in one workspace. Built by permit expediters for contractors managing permitting in-house and the professionals who manage it for others.

Hero buttons: Get Started and Request a Demo.

Do not publish placeholder testimonials, invented performance statistics, or unsupported customer logos.

Use the application’s existing login URL for Sign In. Keep that destination centrally configurable; do not assume a new application subdomain or change existing authentication routes.

5. Solution-page layouts

Use a shared layout for both audience pages: hero → operational challenges → relevant capabilities → screenshot walkthrough → CTA.

For Contractors

Focus on permit coordinators, office managers, operations managers, and project managers.

Lead with project visibility, assignments, document organization, jurisdiction information, and inspection follow-up. Include examples relevant to specialty contractors and general contractors.

For Permit Expediters

Focus on business owners, expediters, coordinators, and accounting staff.

Lead with managing multiple clients, contractor requests, staff responsibilities, contractor-specific pricing, reimbursable expenses, invoicing, and supported QuickBooks Online exports.

6. Signup framework

Create one /signup entry point with two selectable options:
- Contractor / Construction Company
- Permit Expediting Company
Selection should tailor the introductory text and record the prospect’s business type. It should not imply separate product editions or different feature access.

Initial fields
- Full name
- Work email
- Company name
- Business type
- Phone number — optional
- Approximate monthly permit volume — use ranges, including “Not sure”
Include “Already have an account? Sign In.”

Connect the page to the actual onboarding model:
Onboarding model	Submit button	Result
Self-service account creation is supported	Create Company Account	Use the application’s registration, email verification, and company-setup flow.
Account creation requires staff onboarding	Request Access	Save the request and display a clear confirmation explaining the next step.


Build the framework to support either model. Do not show an account-created confirmation unless an account was actually provisioned. Do not advertise a free trial, instant access, or no-credit-card signup until those offers are approved.

Employees joining an existing customer company should use the application’s invitation process rather than accidentally creating another company.

7. Demo and customer sign-in

The demo form should collect name, work email, company, business type, and an optional description of the current permitting challenge. Include clear success and error states.

Customer sign-in should go directly to the application’s existing authentication flow, including its password-reset process. The marketing website should not introduce a separate customer password system.

8. Footer and framework acceptance

Footer: product links, both solution pages, Request a Demo, Get Started, Sign In, Contact, Privacy, and Terms.

The initial framework is ready for review when:
- All pages, navigation links, and CTAs work.
- Both audience paths are clearly represented.
- Signup and demo forms submit to the agreed destination and show accurate confirmations.
- Sign In reaches the correct application login.
- Mobile layouts and accessible form labels are implemented.
- Each public page has an appropriate title, description, and heading structure.
- CTA clicks and successful form submissions are measurable, without sending form contents to analytics.
- Screenshots use sanitized demonstration data.
First review deliverable: a responsive homepage, both solution-page layouts, the feature-page framework, and working signup/demo/sign-in paths. Final pricing, screenshots, and approved legal copy can then be added to that structure.
